## Visual findings

The image presents a complete right-facing shrouded figure, its upright capped funerary post, and its compact integral stone foot. The rounded cap and lower stone foot are intact, with transparent space around the silhouette. The lower robe reaches the base rather than ending at a torso cutoff. Legs are not required for this actor. I see no detached debris, surrounding scenery, blood, wounds, text, cross, or recognizable franchise emblem.

The narrow dark hood opening conceals the face as intended. Its drooping right edge establishes facing. The long pallid arm has a coherent shoulder, elbow, forearm, wrist, and hanging hand, and differs clearly in material from the layered olive cloth. Fine fingers remain distinguishable at this preview size, although their interior detail will require later phone-scale review. The static mass and thick stone foot convey rootedness. The visible post, wrapped bands, ragged cloth, and weathered skin belong to the same matte painted material family as the pursuer. The olive/pallid upper figure separates from the warm leather hunter and cold violet/slate masonry. The compact stone foot itself shares the environment's material appropriately.

Two structural issues prevent locking this as the animation identity:

1. **Bound aiming upper arm.** The large rope bands cross directly over and tightly encircle the exposed upper arm at its middle, pinning it against the torso/post. The raw image makes this especially clear. This is a credible restrained body at rest, but it does not supply a credible free shoulder/upper arm for the specified raised-arm aiming action. Locally rotating that arm would move it through or detach it from those bands; changing the bindings later would change the approved construction. **Correction:** keep strong visible bindings around the torso and post, but route them behind the aiming arm or below the armpit so the upper arm, shoulder, elbow, and hand can lift together without passing through a restraint. Retain the rooted post and complete base.

2. **Post cap is the topmost feature.** The stone finial visibly rises above the hood. `contract/art_contract.json` specifies for `ranged_aim` that the hood stays the top of the silhouette. This identity would force the later clip to violate that statement or change the fixed post/hood relationship. **Correction:** lower the fixed post cap below the hood's highest contour, or raise the hood above the cap while preserving the 300 px complete figure/post/base envelope through bounded uniform normalization. Keep comfortable headroom and full base on the contract pivot.

The first issue is the material animation-readiness blocker; the second is a direct design/contract mismatch. Neither requires changing the successful palette, right-facing pose, concealed face, or overall rooted silhouette.

## Registration and normalization

The provenance records uniform canvas conversion from 1070×1470 to 256×352, followed by bounded uniform normalization 0.967741935483871 from source pivot (128,332) to contract pivot (128,320), targeting 300 px height, with zero debris removed. This declared correction is inside ART_CONTRACT G-03's 0.85–1.18 range and does not describe stretching. Visually the key retains the raw proportions and complete base. This review makes no claim of an independently run pixel audit; registration/debris/transparency automation remains a separate gate.

## Decision

Reject this static key for the named corrections **free the aiming upper arm from the torso bindings** and **place the fixed post cap below the hood apex**. A fresh independent reviewer must inspect the revised candidate. Later animation must still prove actual sway, arm articulation, hand charge shape, fixed base registration, stable identity, and the rest of §9; this static verdict cannot approve those future states.

STATIC KEY REVIEW: FAIL
